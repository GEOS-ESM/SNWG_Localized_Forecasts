Background

Global-scale modeling of air quality and trace gases provides key information in data-sparse regions of importance to federal agencies. Air quality forecasts are necessary for health and safety decisions at U.S. Embassies in countries with poor air quality. Air quality measurements (i.e., nitrogen dioxide — NO₂) are also needed in agriculture and rural regions since current in situ networks are concentrated in urban centers.

NASA's Global Modeling and Assimilation Office (GMAO) integrates data from distributed Pandora sensors into the Goddard Earth Observing System – Composition Forecast (GEOS-CF) model, using downscaling approaches for more precise air quality forecasts at locations of interest. There is a multi-agency need to improve the U.S. government's ability to detect and forecast crucial air quality parameters in poorly sampled locations — both domestic and international.

NASA leads in the validation of satellites and models and is leveraging these capabilities to meet these agency needs. This solution leverages and extends existing NASA sensor networks and forecasting models, and develops new technology to provide more efficient and accurate atmospheric composition forecasting.

Status
Currently in Development — Some Data Available Now

Installation of Pandora sensors is ongoing for both domestic and embassy sites. AERONET and Clarity sensors will also be installed at select embassy sites.

  • PM2.5 forecasts available for +270 embassy locations, and 141 Pandora sites.
  • NO2 forecasts currently available at 141 Pandora sites.
  • O3 forecasts currently being developed.

Solution Characteristics

Distributed Pandora Sensors

Dependent on sensor installation Hourly 141 Pandora sites + 20 embassies + 10 agricultural sites (scalable)

Ground-based Pandora Spectrometer System, AERONET sensors, and Clarity sensors measuring total column ozone, NO2, formaldehyde, and tropospheric profiles of NO2 and formaldehyde as well as surface PM2.5 concentrations.

PM2.5, NO2, O3 Forecasts GEOS-CF Localized Forecasts

Jan 2018 – Present Hourly

Ground-based Pandora Spectrometer System and GEOS-CF model output providing total column and surface ozone, nitrogen dioxide, and formaldehyde. Forecasts available near-real-time; historical data available with 24-hour latency.

JSONText files

PM2.5 Forecasts (GEOS-FP CNN)

May 2024 – present 3-hourly, up to 72 h ahead 270 embassies + 10,000+ cities

GEOS-Forward Processing, VIIRS, and available PM2.5 measurements combined via machine learning to deliver surface PM2.5 concentration and U.S. EPA-defined Air Quality Index. Updated daily; ~2–4 hour latency dependent on GEOS-FP.
